# What actually gets a proposal opened and hired

The panel is seven language models. Left alone, language models reward what they like to read: clever analysis, risk-spotting, long plans. Real Upwork clients skim a list of 20 to 50 proposals and look for two things: **has this person built an app like mine, and can I click it?** This file grounds the panel and the rewrite in what Upwork says, in market data, and in proposals that were actually hired. When it conflicts with a reviewer's taste, this file wins.

## What the client sees before opening a proposal

- Upwork says clients see the freelancer's **profile picture, title, rate and Job Success Score** before opening a proposal, and that with high application volume a client may never reach a proposal at all ([Upwork](https://www.upwork.com/resources/why-are-my-proposals-not-being-viewed)).
- Only the **opening of the cover letter** shows in the list, roughly the first two lines or about 200 characters ([Upwork cover letter guide](https://www.upwork.com/resources/cover-letter-tips), [GigRadar](https://gigradar.io/blog/upwork-proposal-response-rate)).
- Upwork **ranks proposals by match**: it compares the proposal and the profile against the job post (keywords, skills, experience, work history) and shows the closest matches first ([Upwork](https://www.upwork.com/resources/why-are-my-proposals-not-being-viewed)). A cover letter that drops the job's main keywords (the app type, the stack) can rank lower.
- **Boosted proposals** take the top four slots ([Upwork](https://www.upwork.com/resources/how-to-boost-proposal)).
- Screening answers may be **read before the cover letter** ([Upwork Community](https://community.upwork.com/t5/Freelancers/What-do-clients-see-first-Cover-Letter-or-Questions/m-p/828381)).
- The cover letter can be **edited up to 6 hours after sending, or until the client views it**, whichever comes first ([Upwork](https://www.upwork.com/resources/how-to-boost-proposal)). So: send a good version fast, improve it right after.

## What the data says (vendor data, directional only)

- **Speed matters most.** In 59,339 proposals, replies were 11.86% when sent 3 to 4 minutes after the job was posted, against 5.34% at 30 to 45 minutes; the platform mean was 7.45% ([GigRadar](https://gigradar.io/blog/upwork-algorithm)). Vollna also finds a lift for applying within the first hour ([Vollna](https://www.vollna.com/market-insights)).
- **Crowding kills.** Reply rate fell from 9.44% to 2.11% as more competing bids landed on the same job ([GigRadar](https://gigradar.io/blog/upwork-algorithm)).
- **The profile is a bigger lever than the letter.** The same proposal library replied at 1 to 2% on one profile and 14 to 18% on another ([GigRadar](https://gigradar.io/blog/upwork-outreach)).
- **Low view rate means visibility, not wording.** View rate diagnoses the profile card, timing and match; reply rate diagnoses the cover letter ([GigRadar](https://gigradar.io/blog/upwork-proposal-response-rate)). Upwork's **Stats and trends** shows which proposals were viewed ([Upwork Help](https://support.upwork.com/hc/en-us/articles/211062968-How-to-use-your-Upwork-stats-and-trends)).
- **Short wins under $10k.** Short letters are reported to reply far better than long-form on projects under $10k ([GigRadar](https://gigradar.io/blog/upwork-proposal-response-rate)).

All of these figures come from tool vendors analysing their own customers. Use them as direction, not law.

## The proof-first shape (from proposals that were hired)

Real hired proposals for app-development jobs follow this shape. None of them opened with analysis, a risk list or a milestone plan.

1. **Line one names the client's kind of app, in the client's words, and says the freelancer has built it.** "Hi, I've built [membership and fitness / booking / health-tracking] apps before, including …". A short "Hi," is fine. What fails is a self-introduction that never names the client's kind of app, or a clever observation with no proof.
2. **Line two is one hard credential with a number.** For example: led the team behind a product that raised a stated amount of funding and is used by named kinds of customers.
3. **A proof list of 3 to 7 clickable links**, most relevant first, each with a short label: App Store and Play Store listings first, then demo videos (Loom), then screenshots or attachments. Clients click. Links that need a login do not count.
4. **Every explicit ask is answered.** If the post says "To apply, please send: examples, experience with X, timeline, rate, availability", the proposal answers each one, in that order, briefly.
5. **Availability**: when they can start, and hours per day if relevant.
6. **Stack advice only when the post asks for it**, in two or three bullets.
7. **A plain sign-off.** No essay.

Typical length: 60 to 180 words before the links.

What the losing proposals had that the winners did not: a first line with no proof and none of the job's keywords, a long milestone plan or technical essay before any proof, and hedging ("once I have reviewed", "I would confirm later").

## Not opened? Check these before blaming the letter

1. **Timing**: how long after posting did you send? Under an hour is good; minutes is better.
2. **Competition**: how many proposals were already in (the post shows "Proposals: 20 to 50" etc.)?
3. **Profile card**: does your title match this job's kind of app and stack? Is your rate in range? JSS, badges? Use `/upwork-profile-panel` for this.
4. **Boost**: on a job you really want, a boost puts you in the top four.
5. **Match keywords**: do the first lines and your profile contain the job's app type and stack words?
6. **Job quality**: payment verified, client hire rate, budget, how old the post is.
